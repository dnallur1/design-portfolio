Out of Reflection

The clearer it becomes, the less you trust it.

01

Introduce
the game.

Game description

Out of Reflection is a short observation puzzle game exploring the theme of hallucination. The player compares a room with its blurred reflection and clicks objects that do not match. Each correct discovery brings the mirror into focus while changing the remaining mismatches.

Across three continuous stages in the same room, the player notices obvious differences, discovers finer details, and eventually finds that selecting objects in the mirror changes the room itself.

Player experience

The player begins with curiosity and a simple goal: make the reflection clear. As familiar details shift, they question their memory and which version of the room they can trust. The final reflection is sharp, but the room is no longer the one they started in.

WE BECOME WHAT WE BEHOLD

We Become What We Behold is a short game by Nicky Case in which players photograph interactions between circle- and square-shaped characters. The pictures appear on a central news screen, influencing the crowd’s behavior. Through this cycle, the game explores how selective media coverage and audience attention can amplify conflict, fear, and division.

Three meaningful moments.

MOMENT 01

Discovering what counts as “interesting”

What I saw and did

I began playing without knowing what to expect from the camera. This photograph showed an empty part of the scene, and the game responded with “WOWWEE, IT’S NOTHING.”

What seemed possible / what changed

At first, I was exploring what I could photograph. The dismissive caption suggested that capturing any part of the scene would not necessarily produce a meaningful response. It encouraged me to look for noticeable actions or unusual subjects.

What I understood afterward

I began to understand that the game judged my photographs through its captions. I could choose where to point the camera, but its feedback shaped what I looked for next.

MOMENT 02

Trying peace after seeing anger spread

What I saw and did

After photographing conflict and noticing more shapes turning red and appearing angry, I searched for peaceful moments. This image shows a smiling character wearing a heart, with the caption “peace is boring. violence goes viral.”

What seemed possible / what changed

I thought photographing something peaceful might change the crowd’s behavior. However, I noticed no improvement from my attempts. After continuing to search for peaceful subjects, I returned to photographing conflict because those pictures had produced visible changes earlier.

What I understood afterward

I interpreted the response as pressure to prioritize conflict. The game’s feedback influenced my behavior: even when I wanted to highlight peace, conflict seemed to be the way to make something happen.

MOMENT 03

Facing the consequences at the end

What I saw and did

As I continued photographing violence, I observed it growing. The ending then showed framed portraits and candles around a dark television, while a circle and square stood together beneath a red heart.

What seemed possible / what changed

The scene shifted from escalating violence to a still, dark setting that looked like a memorial. The heart brought my attention back to the peaceful connection I had tried to photograph earlier.

What I understood afterward

I interpreted the ending as mourning the consequences of the violence. Looking back, my camera seemed to have participated in the cycle: I photographed conflict, it received attention, and the violence grew. The ending made the earlier dismissal of peaceful moments feel more significant.

PLAYER ACTION → GAME RESPONSE → PLAYER UNDERSTANDING

Eleven player-action events.

  1. 01Discovering how photographs work

    Player action

    I took my first photograph without knowing what to expect.

    →
    Game response / observation

    The game displayed a caption with the picture. As I continued photographing, I noticed the scene changing according to what I captured.

    →
    Player understanding

    My photographs seemed to influence the scene, and the captions shaped how each picture was presented.

  2. 02Capturing conflict between shapes

    Player action

    I photographed an angry circle near a square.

    →
    Game response / observation

    The headline read “CIRCLES HATE SQUARES.” Afterward, I noticed more circles turning red and seeming angry.

    →
    Player understanding

    A picture of one individual’s anger became a statement about an entire group. It seemed to spread hostility.

  3. 03Continuing to seek conflict

    Player action

    I kept searching for conflict and taking photographs.

    →
    Game response / observation

    More shapes changed color and appeared angry as I continued.

    →
    Player understanding

    I began connecting my choice of photographs with the growing tension.

  4. 04Searching for peaceful moments

    Player action

    After noticing the increasing anger, I tried photographing something peaceful.

    →
    Game response / observation

    One photograph received the caption “ya gotta catch ’em doing something interesting…” I noticed no change afterward.

    →
    Player understanding

    This attempt did not produce the visible response I had seen after photographing conflict.

  5. 05Returning to conflict

    Player action

    I continued looking for peaceful moments, but eventually returned to photographing conflict because nothing seemed to change.

    →
    Game response / observation

    Conflict photographs had produced noticeable changes, while my peaceful attempts had not.

    →
    Player understanding

    The feedback made photographing conflict seem like the way to move forward.

  6. 06Photographing a message of unity

    Player action

    I photographed a circle and square with hearts and a sign showing the two shapes together.

    →
    Game response / observation

    The caption read “GIVE THE AUDIENCE WHAT THEY WANT.” I observed no effect on the crowd.

    →
    Player understanding

    Even an explicit message of peace seemed unable to interrupt the growing hostility at this point.

  7. 07Seeing widespread hatred

    Player action

    I photographed several angry circles and squares.

    →
    Game response / observation

    The headline read “ALMOST EVERYONE HATES EVERYONE…”

    →
    Player understanding

    The conflict now appeared to extend across both groups.

  8. 08Trying another subject

    Player action

    I photographed a character wearing a hat.

    →
    Game response / observation

    The caption read “hats ain’t cool no more, haven’t you heard?”

    →
    Player understanding

    The game dismissed this alternative subject, suggesting that what counted as interesting had changed.

  9. 09Witnessing violence begin

    Player action

    I captured a circle pointing a gun at a square wearing a heart.

    →
    Game response / observation

    The caption displayed “…” The following scene showed blood and frightened-looking characters.

    →
    Player understanding

    The conflict had escalated from anger to physical violence.

  10. 10Continuing to photograph violence

    Player action

    I kept capturing the violence.

    →
    Game response / observation

    I observed the violence growing as I photographed it. More blood appeared, and the caption “BE SCARED. BE ANGRY.” appeared repeatedly.

    →
    Player understanding

    My photographs seemed to amplify the violence, making me part of the cycle I was recording.

  11. 11Reaching the ending

    Player action

    After the escalating violence, I reached the final scene.

    →
    Game response / observation

    Framed portraits and candles surrounded a dark television. A circle and square stood together beneath a red heart.

    →
    Player understanding

    I could interpret the scene as a memorial to those lost. The remaining connection between the two shapes contrasted with the hatred shown earlier.
