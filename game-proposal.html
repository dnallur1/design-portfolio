03 / GAME PROPOSAL · WEEK 1 · THEME: HALLUCINATION

Out of Reflection

The clearer it becomes, the less you trust it.

01

Introduce
the game.

Game description

Out of Reflection is a short observation puzzle game exploring the theme of hallucination. The player compares a room with its blurred reflection and clicks objects that do not match. Each correct discovery brings the mirror into focus while changing the remaining mismatches.

Across three continuous stages in the same room, the player notices obvious differences, discovers finer details, and eventually finds that selecting objects in the mirror changes the room itself.

Player experience

The player begins with curiosity and a simple goal: make the reflection clear. As familiar details shift, they question their memory and which version of the room they can trust. The final reflection is sharp, but the room is no longer the one they started in.

WE BECOME WHAT WE BEHOLD

We Become What We Behold is a short game by Nicky Case in which players photograph interactions between circle- and square-shaped characters. The pictures appear on a central news screen, influencing the crowd’s behavior. Through this cycle, the game explores how selective media coverage and audience attention can amplify conflict, fear, and division.

Three meaningful moments.

MOMENT 01

Discovering what counts as “interesting”

What I saw and did

I began playing without knowing what to expect from the camera. This photograph showed an empty part of the scene, and the game responded with “WOWWEE, IT’S NOTHING.”

What seemed possible / what changed

At first, I was exploring what I could photograph. The dismissive caption suggested that capturing any part of the scene would not necessarily produce a meaningful response. It encouraged me to look for noticeable actions or unusual subjects.

What I understood afterward

I began to understand that the game judged my photographs through its captions. I could choose where to point the camera, but its feedback shaped what I looked for next.

MOMENT 02

Trying peace after seeing anger spread

What I saw and did

After photographing conflict and noticing more shapes turning red and appearing angry, I searched for peaceful moments. This image shows a smiling character wearing a heart, with the caption “peace is boring. violence goes viral.”

What seemed possible / what changed

I thought photographing something peaceful might change the crowd’s behavior. However, I noticed no improvement from my attempts. After continuing to search for peaceful subjects, I returned to photographing conflict because those pictures had produced visible changes earlier.

What I understood afterward

I interpreted the response as pressure to prioritize conflict. The game’s feedback influenced my behavior: even when I wanted to highlight peace, conflict seemed to be the way to make something happen.

MOMENT 03

Facing the consequences at the end

What I saw and did

As I continued photographing violence, I observed it growing. The ending then showed framed portraits and candles around a dark television, while a circle and square stood together beneath a red heart.

What seemed possible / what changed

The scene shifted from escalating violence to a still, dark setting that looked like a memorial. The heart brought my attention back to the peaceful connection I had tried to photograph earlier.

What I understood afterward

I interpreted the ending as mourning the consequences of the violence. Looking back, my camera seemed to have participated in the cycle: I photographed conflict, it received attention, and the violence grew. The ending made the earlier dismissal of peaceful moments feel more significant.
